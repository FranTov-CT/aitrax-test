The morning fog rolled across the quiet valley floor.
Scattered birds traced slow arcs against a pale sky.
A rusted gate creaked somewhere beyond the orchard.
Dew clung to every blade of grass in silver beads.
Children laughed in a kitchen two houses away.
The baker turned his sign to open at half past six.
Steam curled from a chimney on the eastern ridge.
An old dog stretched and yawned beside the porch.
Wind carried the scent of pine and distant rain.
The river murmured secrets to the smooth gray stones.
A bicycle leaned against the weathered fence post.
Somewhere a church bell counted out the early hour.
Leaves drifted lazily onto the cobblestone path.
The postman whistled a tune he could not name.
Clouds gathered slowly like travelers at a station.
A cat observed the street from a sunlit sill.
The market stalls unfolded their striped awnings.
Coffee brewed in a dozen sleepy households at once.
The day stretched open like a book never read.
And the valley breathed, patient and unhurried.
