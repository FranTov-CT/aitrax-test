The library smelled of old paper and quiet thought.
Dust motes floated in the amber afternoon light.
A reader turned a page with careful reverence.
Shelves rose like canyons of forgotten knowledge.
Somewhere a clock ticked away the patient hours.
A student slept with his head upon an atlas.
The librarian shelved returns without a sound.
Rain tapped gently at the tall arched windows.
Ink and leather perfumed the narrow aisles.
A map of a vanished empire hung on the wall.
Footsteps echoed softly across the wooden floor.
A whispered conversation drifted between the stacks.
Candles once lit these rooms a century ago.
A moth circled a green-shaded reading lamp.
The oldest book here predated the city itself.
Children gathered for a story at the corner table.
Each volume held a world between its covers.
The afternoon dissolved into golden stillness.
A pen scratched notes in a worn leather journal.
And the library kept its secrets, as always.
