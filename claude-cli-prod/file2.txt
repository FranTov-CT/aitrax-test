Neon signs flickered above the rain-slicked avenue.
Taxis hissed through puddles toward unknown fares.
A saxophone wailed from a basement jazz club door.
Umbrellas bloomed like dark flowers on the corner.
The subway rumbled somewhere beneath the pavement.
Strangers shared a glance and then moved apart.
A vendor sold roasted chestnuts from a steel cart.
Skyscrapers wore crowns of blinking red lights.
Someone argued softly into a cracked phone screen.
The city never truly slept, only blinked slowly.
Puddles mirrored the chaos of a thousand windows.
A busker counted coins in a battered guitar case.
Headlights swept across the faces of the crowd.
The aroma of fried noodles drifted from a stall.
A couple danced beneath a leaking fire escape.
Pigeons huddled under the shelter of an awning.
The clock tower chimed and no one looked up.
Graffiti told stories no gallery would ever hang.
Night folded the streets into shimmering ribbons.
And the pulse of the city carried on, relentless.
